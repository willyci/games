How to play

Jieqi (揭棋, “reveal chess”) is Chinese Chess with hidden pieces. Red (bottom) moves first; against the computer you play Red. Tap a piece, then tap a dotted point to move.
Setup: both Generals start face-up on their usual points. Each side’s other 15 pieces are shuffled and placed face-down on the 15 normal starting points. Face-down pieces are grey discs with a double ring showing 暗: light grey = Red’s, dark grey = Black’s.
Face-down moves: a face-down piece moves like the piece that normally starts on its point (on a cannon point it moves like a cannon, on a soldier point like a soldier, and so on). The Hints button shows that movement type on each face-down disc.
Reveal: when a face-down piece moves (or captures) it is turned face-up where it lands and from then on moves as what it really is. Capturing a face-down enemy shows what it was; captured pieces are listed under the board.
Revealed pieces: 帥/將 General: one step along a line, never leaving the palace; the two generals may never face each other on an open file. 仕/士 Advisor: one step diagonally — once revealed it may go anywhere, not just the palace. 相/象 Elephant: exactly two points diagonally, blocked if the point in between is occupied — once revealed it may cross the river. 傌/馬 Horse: one step straight then one diagonally outward; blocked by a piece next to it in the direction it moves. 俥/車 Chariot: any distance along a line. 炮/砲 Cannon: moves like a chariot, but captures only by jumping over exactly one piece. 兵/卒 Soldier: one step toward the enemy side; after crossing the river it may also step sideways.
Face-down pieces block and screen like any piece, and give check by their face-down movement. You may not leave your general in check. A side with no legal move (checkmate or stalemate) loses. Three-fold repetition or 60 moves each without a capture is a draw. The computer does not peek: it only knows how many of each piece are still face-down. Undo takes back your last move; face-down pieces are reshuffled when you undo, so it cannot be used to peek.
